I'm sorry

My love, I am truly sorry. I hurt you, and I ruined an anniversary that should have made you feel loved, appreciated, and important to me. You deserved better from me. I know saying "I'm sorry" does not undo what happened. I also know that explaining myself right now could sound like I am making excuses. I'm not writing this to defend myself or pressure you to forgive me. I am writing because I need to take responsibility for what I did and for the pain I caused you. I love you, but I have not made you feel loved in the way you needed. I failed to listen, pay attention, and understand how deeply my actions affected you. I am sorry for making you feel like you did not matter—because you do matter to me more than I have shown. Hearing that you want a divorce scares me, but I understand that this is not only about what I want. Your feelings are real, and I will not dismiss them or argue you out of them. If you are willing, I want to listen to you completely—without interrupting, defending myself, or telling you how you should feel. I don't expect one letter or one promise to repair everything. I know trust is rebuilt through actions, not words. If you give me the opportunity, I will do the work, including counseling, changing how I communicate, and proving through consistent actions that I can be a better husband. I am deeply sorry for hurting you. I love you, and I am asking for the chance to listen, understand, and try to repair what I have damaged. Whatever happens next, you deserved this apology without excuses. I'm sorry.

— Malcolm
